Step one

Installing the app

Everything lives on your own phone or browser. There's no account, no server, and no login — each device keeps its own data.

Android

Phone APK

  1. Open nutrition.apk from the download button above on your phone.
  2. When Android warns about installing from an unknown source, allow it — the repo is public, so the file downloads without a GitHub sign-in.
  3. Open the app once installed.
  4. When a newer version is out, the app shows a banner on Android with an Update button linking straight to the latest APK (checked at most once a day).
iPhone & everything else

Web app

  1. Open the web app link above in Safari (or any browser).
  2. On iPhone, tap the Share icon, then Add to Home Screen — it opens like a normal app, no App Store needed. If you skip this, the app shows a reminder banner on Safari; Safari can clear a site's storage after a week or so of not visiting, and the Home Screen app's storage isn't affected by that.
  3. It's the same app and data model, with one gap: steps and workouts need Health Connect, which only exists on Android (see Steps & workouts).
  4. Already using it in Safari with data you want to keep? Export it from Settings first (see Settings & backups), then import it in the installed app — the Home Screen app has separate storage from Safari.
Step two

First-time setup

The first time you open the app, a setup form walks you through this. None of it leaves your device.

  1. 1

    Sex, birth date and height — a Male/Female switch, a date picker (you must be between 13 and 100 years old), and height in cm (100–250).

  2. 2

    Activity level — Sedentary ("Desk job, little or no exercise"), Light ("Light exercise 1–3 days a week"), Moderate ("Exercise 3–5 days a week") or Active ("Hard exercise 6–7 days a week"). Starts on Light. This and the fields above seed your starting calorie estimate (the Mifflin–St Jeor formula) before there's enough history to measure it directly.

  3. 3

    Lose weight or Gain weight, your goal weight in kg, and a weekly rate slider from 0.25% of body weight a week up to a cap the app sets from your height and body composition — up to 1.0% when losing, up to 0.5% when gaining (gaining caps lower: large surpluses mostly add fat, not muscle). Going faster than your cap costs more muscle than fat, so the app won't offer it. A protein slider (1.6–2.2 g per kg, starts at 1.8) and your weekly check-in day (starts on Sunday) round out the form.

  4. 4

    Today's weight, entered right there on the setup screen, plus Health Connect on Android if you want to connect it now (you can also do this later — see Steps & workouts). Tap Save and start, or I'll do this later to skip for now (there's also a Later button in the app bar at the top of this screen that does the same thing).

  5. 5

    Log today and weigh in tomorrow morning. The app needs at least 7 fully-logged days and 6 weigh-ins over a 10-day trend before it can measure your real maintenance calories instead of estimating them — see Adaptive targets.

Today tab

Food logging

Today always shows four meals — Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner and Snacks — plus your calorie card up top. Tap a meal, or its + icon, to add to it.

Adding food

The add-food screen opens on four tabs: Recent, Favorites, My foods and Search, plus two buttons up top — Type it and, if you haven't turned off barcode scanning, Scan.

Recent, Favorites, My foods

Each is a plain list of foods you've used or saved before. Tap one to choose how much (grams, or servings if the food has one). A small circle-plus icon on Recent and Favorites rows re-logs the last amount instantly, with an Undo snackbar.

Search

Typing searches your own saved foods live, under "Your foods" — this part works offline. Below it, a "Search online for ‘…’" button queries Open Food Facts and USDA together and merges the results into one list (Open Food Facts first), each row showing brand and calories per 100 g.

Portion screen

The last step for any food: pick grams or servings, see the calories and macros update live, and tap a star in the app bar to favorite it. It remembers the amount you used last time.

Scanning a barcode

The Scan button opens your camera for EAN/UPC barcodes, with a torch toggle and a "Type barcode" option if the camera can't read it or you'd rather type the digits.

  • Found it — goes straight to the portion screen.
  • Lookup failed (offline, rate-limited) — a dialog offers "Try again" or "Scan again".
  • Not in the database — a "Not found" dialog offers "Add from label", which opens the add-from-label form with the barcode already filled in.

Describe what you ate

The Type it button opens a text box. Type a meal the way you'd actually say it — "5 spoons of cottage cheese 5% and 2 eggs" or "chicken breast 200g, 1 cup rice" — and it's parsed a third of a second after you stop typing, no account or API key, entirely offline against a built-in list of common and Israeli foods plus anything you've added yourself.

Each food becomes a card: the matched name (tap to change it), an amount chip like "5 tbsp · 65 g" (tap to adjust), the estimated calories, and a short "why" line explaining the estimate. Anything it couldn't match shows as "Didn't catch ‘…’" with a "Pick a food" or "Search online" option. Swipe a card away to drop it, then tap "Add N items" to log everything at once.

It learns two things, the moment you correct them: picking a different food for a phrase remembers that mapping next time, and typing an explicit gram amount for a spoon, cup or piece remembers your spoon size for that food. There's no separate "training" step — it just happens as you use it.

Add from a label

No match anywhere? Add the food yourself: name, optional brand, a Per 100 g / Per serving toggle, serving size and name if you use one, then energy, protein, fat and carbs — the two bases convert automatically as you switch. It's saved to My foods for next time. Reachable from the + button on the add-food screen, from a failed barcode lookup, or from "Create food" while describing a meal.

Copying and editing days

A copy icon by the meals card's total opens "Copy whole day from yesterday" or "…from another day" (any date up to 3 years back). Each meal also has its own ⋮ menu with the same two options for just that meal, and an empty meal gets a one-tap "Same as yesterday" chip when yesterday had something logged there. Every copy shows a 5-second Undo snackbar.

The calendar icon in Today's app bar jumps to any day in the last 3 years to fix or fill in its meals; the arrows beside it step one day at a time and stop at today, and tapping the date in the middle jumps straight back to today. Swipe a logged item left to delete it (also undo-able), or tap it to edit the amount or move it to a different meal.

"Day fully logged"

A switch at the bottom of the meals card, subtitled "Counts toward your weekly check-in". This is more than a label: only days you've marked fully logged are used to measure your real maintenance calories at the weekly check-in, so a day you forgot to log dinner won't quietly drag your numbers down. If you logged something yesterday but never flagged it, Today shows a one-time "Was yesterday complete?" prompt with a quick "Yes, mark it" option.

Weight tab

Daily weigh-ins

One weigh-in per day. The app smooths your entries into a trend line, so a salty dinner or a bad night's sleep doesn't look like real progress or a setback — only the trend feeds your targets.

Logging a weigh-in

The fastest way is the Morning weigh-in card that sits right on the Today tab, above the calorie card, whenever you haven't logged today's weight yet — type the number and tap Save, no need to switch tabs. You can also add or edit any day's weigh-in from the Weight tab itself: the Weigh-in button at the bottom (it becomes Update today once you've already logged today) opens the same dialog, with a date picker ("Tap to change the date") so you can log a day you missed.

Trend, changes and chart

The summary card at the top shows your current trend weight, its change over the last 7 and 30 days, and (if you've set a goal weight) how far you still have to go. Below it, the chart has three range chips — 30 d, 90 d and All — to zoom the trend line in or out.

Weigh-in history

Below the chart, every weigh-in is listed newest first, each showing its date, weight and the change from the previous entry. Tap one to edit it in the same dialog used to log a new one, or tap its delete icon to remove it (with an Undo snackbar, same as deleting a logged food). The list starts short and shows a "Show all (N more)" button once there's more history than fits.

Weekly check-in

Adaptive targets

Once a week, on the day you chose at setup (Sunday by default), the check-in compares what you actually ate on fully-logged days against how your trend weight moved, and offers new numbers.

A banner on Today when it's due, or Settings → Weekly check-in

It estimates your real maintenance calories from that comparison, then recommends calories plus protein, fat and carbs for the rate you chose — a deficit if you're losing, a surplus if you're gaining. Only days marked "Day fully logged" count, since a day where you forgot to log dinner would make it look like you eat less than you really do.

The check-in screen shows a table — Current, New and the change — for calories, protein, fat, carbs and your maintenance estimate, plus a "Why" section explaining the numbers in plain language. Tap Accept to switch to the new targets, or Skip, keep my current targets to stay put this week. Nothing changes until you tap Accept, and maintenance itself moves by at most 150 kcal a week, so targets shift gradually rather than swinging.

Your current targets also sit on the Settings screen (calories, protein/fat/carbs, and whether they're a formula estimate or based on your own data) and on Today's calorie card, which shows calories remaining (or over) and a progress bar per macro.

Today & Dashboard

Steps & workouts

Android only

Read from Health Connect, the step and workout store built into Android 14 and newer. Health Connect doesn't exist on the web, so this section and its charts are hidden there.

Settings → Health Connect

The tile's button changes with your state: Install or Update Health Connect if it's missing, Connect to grant steps/workouts/distance/calories access, then Allow full history — a second permission that lets the first sync reach back 90 days instead of 30. Sync now is always there once connected.

Steps appear on Today with a progress bar toward your goal, and as a bar chart with a 7-day average line on the Dashboard. They start counting as soon as Health Connect can read them — your old step counter app doesn't need to share anything, though the two counts may differ slightly.

Workouts sync from Hevy once you turn on Health Connect syncing inside Hevy itself; they appear on Today as "<name> · <duration>" with "from Hevy" and the calories underneath, and as a per-week count on the Dashboard. Sync runs whenever the app opens, on Dashboard pull-to-refresh, or from the Health Connect tile's Sync now button.

Logging a workout by hand

The + "Add exercise" button on the Activity card opens a form. Pick an activity — treadmill/outdoor walk or run, cycling, elliptical, swimming, rowing, or Other for anything else (which just takes a name and a calorie figure you type in). Walks and runs take distance, speed and incline, each deriving the others; walking caps at 8 km/h and running at 30 km/h. Everything else uses your body weight (prefilled from your last weigh-in) plus a standard activity-intensity estimate. Whatever the method, the calculated calories are always editable if you'd rather enter your own.

Step goal & walk reminder

Both live in Settings, right under Health Connect. The step goal is a slider from 3,000 to 20,000 (default 10,000). The walk reminder is a toggle that, once on, lets you set a time and a step threshold (1,000–15,000) — it's meant to nudge you if you're under that many steps by that time of day.

Steps and workouts are shown for context only; they're never added back to your calorie budget.

Recipes tab

Recipes

A grid of recipes grouped by meal — Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snacks — with search and filters, plus a Recommended for you row at the top.

The filter button opens max calories, min protein, max carbs and max fat fields — applied live as you type, with a Clear button. The search box matches by name or ingredient.

Recommended for you looks at what's left of today's calorie and macro targets and ranks every recipe by how well it fits what's left, showing the top 5. It's hidden until you have a profile and targets set up.

Tap any recipe for a sheet with its ingredients, numbered steps, and full macros per serving.

Dashboard tab

Dashboard

Five charts, in order, over the time range you pick at the top:

  • Weight — your trend line against your goal.
  • Average intake per week — a bar for what you ate against a bar for your target, fully-logged days only.
  • Steps per day — daily bars with a 7-day average line. Android only
  • Workouts per week — a bar per week. Android only
  • Maintenance estimate — one point per weekly check-in, so you can see the engine's estimate of your maintenance calories move over time.

Until you have a profile and a first weigh-in, this screen shows a "Finish setup" card instead.

Settings

Settings & backups

Your current targets sit at the top once you have a profile, followed by your profile and the weekly check-in. On Android, three more rows follow: Health Connect, step goal and walk reminder — absent on the web app, since they don't apply there. Last comes Your data, with Export data and Import data, on both Android and the web app.

Editing your profile

Your profile locks as soon as it exists — it's read-only by default, to keep the calorie engine's history consistent. Tap Edit to unlock every field, make your changes, and you'll be asked to confirm before they're saved; canceling discards them and re-locks the form.

Export data

Settings → Export data

Writes your entire database — profile, weigh-ins, food log, target history, everything — to a JSON file named nutrition-export-<date>.json and opens the share sheet so you can save it or send it anywhere. On iPhone, pick Save to Files. Browsers that can't share files download it instead. Your data lives only on your phone, so export now and then and keep the file somewhere safe.

Import data

Settings → Import data

Pick an export file and the app shows when it was made and how many weigh-ins and logged foods it holds. Tap Replace to restore it. This replaces everything on this device: anything logged here that isn't in the file is gone. If the file isn't a backup, or anything goes wrong, nothing is changed. Backups from older versions of the app import fine; a backup from a newer version asks you to update the app first.

Setting up a new phone? The Get started screen has Restore from a backup, so you can skip the profile and pick up where you left off.

Report a problem

Settings → Report a problem

Describe what went wrong and tap Send. It's sent along with your app version and platform so the developer doesn't have to ask. No account needed.

Troubleshooting

Common questions

I don't see steps, workouts or the Activity card.

That's expected on the web app — Health Connect only exists on Android, so this card and the two activity charts on the Dashboard are hidden there entirely.

My profile fields are greyed out.

That's normal once a profile exists — it locks itself to keep your target history consistent. Tap Edit to unlock it, then confirm when you save.

My weekly check-in isn't showing up.

It becomes due on your chosen check-in weekday (Sunday by default), and stays due until you open it — look for the banner on Today or the badge on Settings → Weekly check-in. It also needs a profile and enough weigh-in history to have anything new to recommend.

A day I logged isn't counting toward my targets.

Only days switched on as "Day fully logged" (at the bottom of the meals card) feed the weekly check-in's maintenance estimate. A logged-but-unflagged day is skipped on purpose, so an incomplete day doesn't skew the numbers.

Scanning a barcode didn't find my product.

Open Food Facts is crowd-sourced, so not everything is in there. The app will offer to add it yourself from its nutrition label — it's then saved for next time, barcode included.

Can I move my data to a new phone?

Yes. On the old phone, Settings → Export data and save the file. On the new one, open the app and tap Restore from a backup on the Get started screen (or Settings → Import data) and pick that file. Each phone keeps its own data, by design — there's no account or server behind any of this, so the file is your only copy.

Food data from Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central. Full technical details, the calorie engine's formulas, and the source code are in the GitHub repo.
